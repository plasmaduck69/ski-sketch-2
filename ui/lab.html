How the engine works (and what it can't tell you)

Slicing the ski

The ski is cut into 241 cross-sections from tail to tip. Each one gets its real width (from the sidecut circle through the tail contact, waist and tip contact) and its core thickness (from your taper), then the full stack: base, steel edges, rubber, laminates, metal, core with sidewalls and topsheet.

Stiffness

Bending stiffness (EI) uses the transformed-section method: every layer's modulus times its area and its distance from the neutral axis squared. Layers far from the middle (laminates, metal, edges) matter most, which is why a 0.5 mm metal sheet can add as much stiffness as millimeters of wood. Torsional stiffness (GJ) comes from each layer's shear modulus and its distance from the middle, the same way.

Fabrics

Each fabric's areal weight is turned into cured thickness using fiber density and fiber volume fraction (Vf). Its stiffness comes from micromechanics (rule of mixtures and Halpin–Tsai) and classical lamination theory over the ply angles. ±45° plies add little bending stiffness but most of the torsional stiffness, so triax and biax control edge hold.

Whole-ski numbers

Center flex is a three-point bend between the camber contact points. The first vibration mode uses the Rayleigh method. Damping is the strain energy each layer stores in that mode times its loss factor, plus a constrained-layer term for rubber sitting under stiff layers. "Carve energy" is the energy stored bending the running length from its cambered shape into a 15 m arc, which is what the ski gives back at the end of a carved turn.

Scores

Attributes (1–5) place these physics numbers on ranges set by six production skis, modeled with their published dimensions and weight. Use-case fits use the same attribute weights as your planning sheet. Performance is the plain average of the six fits: every use case counts equally, so a weak spot can't hide behind a strong one. On the Projects tab, each iteration's "what changed and why" tests every change on its own against the version it came from. Build quality starts at 10 and loses 2 / 1 / 0.4 for each High / Medium / Low issue. Overall is the average of Performance and Build quality.

Limits

Material values are textbook averages (USDA Wood Handbook, manufacturer data sheets); real wood varies by about ±20%. Production layups are approximations of the published construction, with the underfoot core thickness solved so the modeled weight matches the published weight. Use the numbers to compare designs against each other, not as exact predictions. Damping is the least certain output.
